SG

The SG Design System: Context

Design systems reflect the companies they're built for. The SG Design System covers over 500 products, focused on SG Markets, the bank's platform of financial applications. It's used by around 1000 developers and multiple design teams worldwide.

The system has to cover everything from marketing pages to dense trading apps (see images). It also covers all of the wildly different technical setups that projects have.

Generations

Over 12 years the system has been through several major generations, each with their challenges. I joined SG to join in the transition from v3 to v4. This was a major redesign and my main role was building UI kits from scratch, first in Adobe XD and then in Figma.

To justify the next evolution I leveraged business needs for more complex components and colour issues (read about those here) to have a more major overhaul. We built a whole new v5 of the system and began the 2 year migration plan, with new advanced web components and design patterns.

Today we're working on the 6th generation — a more complete rebuild with AI at the core. Read more about that in 0/1 here.

The numbers

I don't put much store in stats like the number of variants or Figma usage, but it was impressive to see over two million table cell components inserted this year.

Strategy

Small, sustainable teams have always been my go-to. The SG design system working group has never exceeded 5 members, relying on fast decision making, expert contributors and regular reviews of setup/organisation. I'm proud that we've survived multiple funding cut threats (ask me about the most recent) and continued to deliver recognised value.

I believe that everyone on the design systems should also be working directly on product teams too. It avoids system siloes and enables better collaboration and visibility.

A lot of the work that we've enabled has been via a foot in the door strategy — adding additional updates into strategically important work.

Tooling and tech wise I've found it best to try to keep as up to date as possible on the design side, and cater to whatever engineering teams decide that they want. The SG system has been tech agnostic intentionally to serve all the different stacks, but now as teams converge on React we have opened up a strong opportunity to evolve and focus.

Build

Design lab

Design lab is an award winning design/code tool that I designed and help build internally, and scaled to 2,000 users. The tool is simple in that it just gives you an editor to write HTML and then a live preview of your code with the SG Design System applied. I created it first as a tool for the design system team to test new components, but it quickly became the place for sharing templates and snippets, especially after I built features like the component editor and inspect tools.

This kind of tooling has helped break down barriers for non-developers to start coding. Most of the CIB design team now use Design lab instead of making specifications.

The secondary effect was how much I learnt from being a product owner — from budget justifications to product lifecycle and compliance.

Value

The system's value is compounding over time. Initiatives like business patterns (design patterns linked to operational business flows) have been found to cover up to 80% reusability, while the knowledge store is now enabling AI skills to be built which has almost removed any barriers to entry for people wanting to build (more on this in 0/1).

I'll finish with some reflections. First, there's not much I'd change about our actual evolution, as I'm convinced that we mostly took the best paths for the system at the time. I don't regret not adopting design tokens; our solution was as effective and took a tenth of the time.

We should have worked out how to quickly share design patterns and templates; even a basic gallery would have been great. Documentation is always great to fall back on, but active examples are far more effective (albeit time consuming).

Design system tutorial sessions were hugely effective. UI drift is inevitable and needs constant care, especially with flexible systems. Design systems are a utility and a good marker of success is consumers treating them as one.
